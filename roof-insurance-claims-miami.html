How to Handle a Roof Insurance Claim in Miami

Storm damage can leave Miami homeowners asking, “Will insurance cover my roof?” The answer depends on the policy, cause of loss, exclusions and facts of the claim. A roofing contractor can document visible roof conditions, but your insurer determines coverage under your policy.

What to do after roof damage

  1. Put safety first. Do not climb onto an unstable or wet roof.
  2. Prevent further damage when safe. Florida's Department of Financial Services advises homeowners to make emergency repairs such as tarping when needed and to keep receipts.
  3. Document the condition. Take photos or video from safe locations and keep a record of damaged property.
  4. Notify your insurer. Follow the notice requirements in your policy and keep copies of claim communications.
  5. Arrange a professional roof assessment. A contractor can document visible damage and identify repair or replacement needs.

Florida insurance claim timing

Florida's Department of Financial Services says current law generally bars certain hurricane or windstorm claims unless notice is given within one year, with supplemental claims generally allowed within 18 months, subject to the law and policy. The same consumer guidance says insurers generally must pay or deny a property claim within 60 days after notice, subject to exceptions in the law.

Because insurance rules and policies can change, verify the current requirements with the Florida Department of Financial Services and your insurer.

What a roofing inspection can document

  • Visible missing, cracked or displaced roofing materials
  • Water entry and visible interior staining
  • Damage around flashing, penetrations and roof edges
  • Signs of wind or storm-related impact
  • Repair versus replacement recommendations based on observed condition

Common questions

How long do I have to file a roof insurance claim in Florida?

Current Florida consumer guidance describes a one-year notice period for certain hurricane or windstorm claims and 18 months for supplemental claims, subject to applicable law and policy terms. Confirm your specific situation with your insurer and the Florida Department of Financial Services.

Should I repair my roof before the adjuster arrives?

Take reasonable emergency measures to prevent additional damage when safe, document the condition before and after the temporary work, and keep receipts. Avoid permanent repairs that could remove evidence before your insurer has a chance to inspect unless necessary for safety.

Can a roofer decide whether my claim is covered?

No. A roofer can describe roof damage and repair scope. Coverage is determined under the insurance policy and applicable law.
